# Hair Conditions and Common Concerns

## Hair Fall

Hair shedding is a normal part of the hair growth cycle. However,
noticeably increased or sudden hair loss can have many possible
causes.

### Possible Factors

- Genetics
- Stress
- Illness
- Nutritional deficiencies
- Hormonal changes
- Certain medications
- Tight hairstyles
- Excessive chemical or heat exposure

### General Self-Care

- Avoid very tight hairstyles.
- Handle wet hair gently.
- Avoid excessive heat styling.
- Maintain a balanced diet.
- Get adequate sleep.
- Manage prolonged stress where possible.
- Avoid unnecessary chemical treatments.

Sudden, severe, patchy, or persistent hair loss should be
evaluated by a qualified healthcare professional.


---

## Dry and Brittle Hair

Dry hair may feel rough, frizzy, or difficult to manage.
Hair can become brittle when it is exposed to excessive heat,
chemical treatments, friction, or environmental stress.

### General Self-Care

- Reduce frequent heat styling.
- Avoid excessive chemical treatments.
- Handle wet hair gently.
- Avoid aggressive brushing.
- Use conditioner appropriately.
- Protect hair from excessive environmental exposure.


---

## Dandruff and Flaking

Scalp flaking can have several causes. Dandruff is commonly
associated with scalp inflammation and changes involving
Malassezia yeast.

### General Self-Care

- Keep the scalp clean according to individual needs.
- Avoid scratching the scalp.
- Avoid sharing combs, brushes, or personal hair items.
- Avoid applying irritating substances to the scalp.
- Seek professional advice if symptoms persist or become severe.


---

## Oily Scalp

An oily scalp can occur when the sebaceous glands produce
higher amounts of sebum.

### Possible Factors

- Genetics
- Hormonal changes
- Environmental conditions
- Individual scalp characteristics
- Haircare practices

### General Self-Care

- Wash the scalp according to its oiliness and activity level.
- Avoid excessive scratching.
- Keep hair and scalp clean.
- Avoid applying unnecessary heavy substances to the scalp.


---

## Itchy Scalp

An itchy scalp may result from dryness, irritation, dandruff,
allergic reactions, or other scalp conditions.

### General Self-Care

- Avoid scratching.
- Avoid products that repeatedly cause irritation.
- Keep the scalp clean.
- Avoid applying harsh DIY substances.
- Observe whether a particular product consistently triggers
  symptoms.

Persistent, severe, painful, swollen, or infected-looking scalp
symptoms should be evaluated professionally.


---

## Split Ends

Split ends occur when the protective outer layer of the hair
becomes damaged and the hair fiber separates.

### Contributing Factors

- Excessive heat
- Chemical treatments
- Mechanical damage
- Rough brushing
- Friction
- Environmental exposure

### Prevention

- Reduce excessive heat.
- Handle hair gently.
- Avoid aggressive brushing.
- Keep chemical treatments limited.
- Regular trimming can remove existing split ends.

Split ends cannot be permanently repaired once the hair fiber
has split.


---

## Frizzy Hair

Frizz can occur when hair fibers absorb moisture from the
environment or when the hair cuticle becomes damaged.

### Helpful Habits

- Handle hair gently.
- Avoid excessive heat.
- Avoid aggressive towel drying.
- Use conditioner appropriately.
- Reduce unnecessary chemical treatments.
- Protect hair from excessive environmental exposure.


---

## Damaged Hair

Repeated exposure to heat, chemicals, friction, and environmental
stress can weaken the hair shaft.

### Prevention

- Reduce frequent heat styling.
- Use lower heat when heat styling is necessary.
- Avoid excessive bleaching or chemical processing.
- Detangle gently.
- Avoid very tight hairstyles.
- Maintain adequate nutrition.


---

## When to Seek Professional Advice

Professional evaluation is appropriate for:

- Sudden or rapid hair loss
- Patchy hair loss
- Persistent scalp pain
- Severe itching
- Bleeding or sores on the scalp
- Significant inflammation
- Signs of infection
- Hair loss accompanied by other concerning symptoms
- Persistent symptoms that do not improve
